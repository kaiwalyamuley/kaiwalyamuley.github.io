    How it works

    One real ball, from the scoreboard to its Pressure Index, then from Pressure Index to player rating.

    1. ScoreboardRuns needed, balls left, wickets in hand
    2. Win chanceA model estimates who is winning before each ball
    3. StakesHow far could the next ball swing the match?
    4. Pressure IndexStakes, closeness, stage and momentum, 0 to 100
    5. RatingValue above expected on pressure balls, fairly adjusted

    The rating, step by step

    1. Compare with the situation

    Each ball's value is runs scored minus the cost of a wicket, compared with the average in the same competition, innings, phase, wickets lost and pressure state.

    2. Weigh it fairly

    Older balls and weaker opposition count for less. Small samples are pulled toward what the player's ordinary form predicts, so a hot streak cannot top the list alone.

    3. Blend five parts

    Rating = 40% pressure value, 20% uplift over own level, 15% win impact, 15% consistency, 10% extreme-pressure value. Scaled so 50 is average.

    Eligibility: at least 100 pressure balls for batters or 120 for bowlers, 15 pressure innings or spells, 150 ordinary balls, and pressure balls in at least 2 calendar years.
